# Season and career metric methodology

The pinned data covers results through 2024-07-07. The indices are reconstructed exploratory comparisons, not recovered original formulas or objective measures of talent or reliability. The incomplete 2024 season is excluded from championship totals. A year is complete only when every scheduled race has a result; champions must have position 1 in standings at that year's final scheduled race. A driver's title is attributed to their constructor with the most earned driver points that season, with ties resolved by distinct race wins, result entries, then constructor ID.

Constructor/year summaries include every recorded constructor-season. Latest available cumulative official standings supply points and position; where unavailable, result points and dense earned-points rank are reconstructed. Race points use official constructor results when available and driver result sums otherwise. Average points when scoring uses positive-point recorded team races. Scoring rate is the fraction of eligible team-races with positive race points, and is null when no team-race has an eligible entry. Finish rate uses finished eligible entries divided by eligible entries; podium rate uses eligible entries with numeric positions 1–3 divided by eligible entries. Nonstarts are excluded from entry rates. Average finish and consistency use classified numeric positions among eligible entries, and consistency is 1/(1+population standard deviation); no classified positions yields null. Winning races are distinct, and dominance is winning races divided by recorded team races. Driver contributions divide earned driver points by all earned driver points in the team-season, not penalized official constructor points; zero denominators yield null. Extended rows repeat season metrics per driver and must not be summed as independent team-seasons.

The driver career index includes only drivers with at least 20 distinct eligible driver-races. Components on 0–100 scale are: 100 times (0.6 win rate + 0.4 podium rate); titles relative to the maximum; classified driver-races divided by eligible driver-races; eligible driver-races relative to the maximum; winning constructors relative to the maximum; mean comparable ordered teammate race result (ties half; both unclassified excluded); mean earned driver share of positive team-race driver points; and the starts-weighted mean of each year's driver win rate divided by the maximum driver win rate in that year. Years with no winner contribute zero. The equal mean of eight components defaults undefined components to zero. Team-points share is a proxy and does not causally adjust for car performance.

Constructor career totals count distinct winning, podium and grid-P1 races, not driver rows. Grid P1 is a starting-grid proxy, not official qualifying poles. Win percentage divides distinct winning races by distinct eligible team races. The field `reliability_score_100` is the percentage of eligible entries with a numeric classified position, and must be labeled classified completion percentage. The final equal mean combines win percentage, classified completion percentage, and win, constructor-title and attributed driver-title counts each normalized to their respective maximum. Undefined components default zero.

Teammate data contains both ordered orientations for each pair of distinct drivers in an eligible team-race. Repeated driver entries have points summed; the representative result is the best numeric classified position, then best position order and result ID, and its positive grid is used. Qualifying compares the best raw qualifying position for each driver/team/race and is null if either is missing; ties score 0.5. Race comparison uses numeric classifications, with a classified driver beating an unclassified driver, equal positions scoring 0.5 and both unclassified yielding null. Grid and race gaps are B minus A, points difference is A minus B, and `Points_Share_Pct` is the 0–100 percentage `100*A/(A+B)`, null for zero denominator (for example, A = 1 and B = 2 gives 33.3333%). Codes fall back to driver references. Page selectors must default to one valid ordered pair to avoid double counting orientations.
